# Exam map & study plan

This track prepares you for **ISTQB Certified Tester Foundation Level (CTFL) v4.0**, the entry certificate of the ISTQB scheme and the prerequisite for every specialist certificate, including CT-AI and CT-GenAI. The current technical baseline is the v4.0.1 errata release of the April 2023 syllabus.

## Exam facts to memorise

- **Questions:** 40 multiple-choice questions, one point each.
- **Pass mark:** 26 of 40 points (65%).
- **Time:** 60 minutes, or 75 minutes when the exam is not in your native language.
- **Prerequisite:** none.
- **Validity:** the certificate does not expire.
- **Official practice:** ISTQB publishes four sample exams (A, B, C and D) with answer sheets.

## What is examinable

Every question maps to a learning objective with a cognitive level:

- **K1 Remember:** recall a term or list.
- **K2 Understand:** explain, compare, classify.
- **K3 Apply:** use a technique on a scenario, such as deriving boundary values or a three-point estimate.

K3 objectives sit mostly in chapter 4 (test techniques) and chapter 5 (estimation, prioritisation, defect reports). They are where calculation questions come from, so practise them by hand.

## The six chapters

| Chapter | Topic | Where the exam leans |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fundamentals of testing | Principles, test activities, testware, roles |
| 2 | Testing throughout the SDLC | Shift left, test levels and types, maintenance testing |
| 3 | Static testing | Review process, roles and review types |
| 4 | Test analysis and design | The heaviest chapter: EP, BVA, decision tables, state transitions, coverage |
| 5 | Managing the test activities | Estimation, prioritisation, risk, monitoring, defect reports |
| 6 | Test tools | Tool categories, benefits and risks of automation |

## How to use this track

1. Read the six chapters in order; chapters 4 and 5 deserve double time.
2. Work every calculation example in chapter 4 on paper.
3. Take the 40-question mock exam under a 60-minute timer.
4. Take ISTQB's official sample exams A to D, then reread the final review.

A realistic pace is three weeks at about an hour a day for someone already working in QA.
